## Project Overview

This project is an interactive digital inquiry created for **Assignment #1: Seeing Myself (A Social Identity Inquiry)** in Social Justice 12.

Instead of presenting surface-level labels, this project explores six core social identities: Socio-Economic Status, Religion & Spirituality, First Language & National Origin, Physical Ability, Age, and Gender. Each chapter brings together two distinct pieces of personal evidence from my life, exploring the deeper complications that are not obvious from a photograph, and connecting my lived experiences directly to the concepts, activities, and frameworks we studied in class.

## Interactive Features

1. **Audio Reflection with Live Waveform Canvas:**

   - Synthesizes an acoustic tone with a live audio visualizer rendered on an HTML5 canvas.

   - Includes a full transcript of my spoken personal reflection.

2. **Chapter-Specific Interactive Lenses:**

   - **Chapter 1:** Labor & Perspective Slider balancing hardware research against household financial reality, with a live CPU frequency readout.

   - **Chapter 2:** Faith Lens Toggle comparing family traditions with personal philosophical skepticism.

   - **Chapter 3:** Telugu Sound Simulation illustrating native vowel pronunciation.

   - **Chapter 4:** Post-Op Squint Blur Simulator demonstrating temporary optical blurriness and reliance on caregivers.

   - **Chapter 5:** Autonomy Paradox Toggle contrasting society's driver's license milestone with gradual family maturity.

   - **Chapter 6:** Gender Expectations Lens contrasting childhood frugality with rigid modern masculine stereotypes.

3. **Dynamic Intersectionality Matrix:**

   - Based on Kimberlé Crenshaw's framework from our class presentation.

   - Allows users to select any two identities to examine their unique compounded friction in my life and connection to class concepts. Covers all 15 pairwise combinations.

4. **Accessible Evidence Details Modal:**

   - Built using the native HTML `<dialog>` element for full keyboard and screen-reader accessibility.

   - Displays detailed background context and rubric notes for each piece of evidence.

### Topic 1: Socio-Economic Status (Class & Wealth)

- **Personal Evidence:** Custom PC Build Boxes (Intel i7-13700K & RTX 4070 SUPER) and Windows 11 Task Manager / specs readout (`Kushal_Desktop`).

- **Surface Perception / Interpretation:** Having a PC of that spec means people assume you are "rich as hell." Driven by me wanting to build a PC since Grade 8.

- **Hidden Complication:** How much my dad had to work to buy me that PC, and how much time I spent completely obsessed about building it. For over a year, I spent almost all free time watching build videos and hunting deals. Holding the boxes brought excitement mixed with quiet guilt on a single-income family budget in Metro Vancouver.

- **Class Slide Connections:**

  * **Effort vs. Result discussion:** Society tells us effort automatically equals result, but meritocracy ignores starting barriers.

  * `EQUITY.pptx`: "Meritocracy disregards systemic advantages and disadvantages. People from different backgrounds do not start out from the same level... but are inherently privileged by economic leverage."

  * "The Scholarship" class activity: Debating whether success is pure talent or family support and barriers.

  * `ACCESS.pptx`: Financial barriers to digital tools; baseball fence visual (Equality vs. Equity vs. Justice).

  * `Why Injustice_.pptx`: Maslow's hierarchy (basic material security vs. psychological growth).

- **Personal Shift:** Moving from viewing technology as an individual achievement to realizing that consumer goods often mask parental labor and economic vulnerability.

- **Wondering Question (from draft sheet):** "How to create a society where effort = result?"

### Topic 2: Religion & Spirituality

- **Personal Evidence:** Me being bald (childhood tonsure ceremony at ancestral temple, age 2) and me at various temples (Tirumala Tirupati pilgrimage, Dec 30, 2024, age 15 in traditional dhoti with parents).

- **Surface Perception / Interpretation:** Connects me to my religion through the various traditions followed by my family across generations.

- **Hidden Complication:** "When I was younger my parents were pretty high on religious belief and that rubbed off on me. But as I started viewing the world logically, I slowly shifted toward an atheist mindset (not fully)." Yet I proudly participate in rituals out of love and respect for my parents who sacrificed everything to immigrate.

- **Class Slide Connections:**

  * **Age impacts maturity of belief, experience, and viewpoint due to religion:** How cognitive growth shifts us from passive absorption to critical questioning, and how life experience reshapes faith.

  * `DIVERSITY.pptx`: Three Dimensions of Diversity: Demographic (born Hindu), Experiential (temple ceremonies and migration), Cognitive (analytical scientific skepticism).

  * "Mirrors vs. Windows": Seeing cultural heritage reflected in temple spaces vs. peering into other belief systems.

  * `HUMAN RIGHTS.pptx`: UDHR (1948) & Eleanor Roosevelt, Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms: Freedom of conscience as a private liberty; rights come with responsibilities to respect family beliefs.

  * `Why Injustice_.pptx`: Maslow's hierarchy: Psychological need for belonging and family connection against feeling isolated in Canada.

- **Personal Shift:** Shifting from early childhood belief, through an argumentative phase, to finding that fine point where religion and logic coexist: logic explains the physical world, while ritual serves as an emotional language of family care and cultural solidarity.

- **Wondering Question (from draft sheet):** "Is there a fine point where religion and logic can co-exist?"

### Topic 3: First Language & National Origin

- **Personal Evidence:** Old textbooks (Standard 4 Tamil Literature and Grammar from Chennai schooling) and me when I came to Canada (2018 YVR airport arrival, reuniting with my dad who came to Canada in 2016).

- **Surface Perception / Interpretation:** My dad came to Canada in 2016, and me and my mom came to Canada in 2018 after two full years of separation. Shows me learning multiple languages across my life (Telugu at home, Tamil in Chennai schooling, English in Canada) to communicate with people.

- **Hidden Complication:** "Couldn't read or write in Mother tongue (Telugu)" because schooling in Chennai was in Tamil and English; and "Treated differently" in Canada, facing constant assumptions that all Indians speak Hindi or Punjabi.

- **Class Slide Connections:**

  * **Judgement about first language:** How society judges and stereotypes people based on their accent, mother tongue, or linguistic background.

  * `Social Identity.pptx` (Slide 10): Ethnicity (shared South Indian culture, language, ancestry) vs. National Origin (Indian by birth, Canadian citizen by political borders).

  * `Privilege and Oppression.pptx`: Stereotypes ("all South Asians speak Hindi") leading to Prejudice, Discrimination, and Erasure. Slide 28: Privilege as ignorance (dominant language speakers never face language dismissal).

  * `DIVERSITY.pptx`: Tokenism vs. true demographic and experiential diversity.

- **Personal Shift (from draft sheet):** Adopting the "Framework of effort": realizing that knowing more languages rather than perfecting only one lets me communicate with and understand far more people, while honoring my mother's two years of solo parenting in Chennai.

- **Wondering Question / Shift (from draft sheet):** "Knowing more languages rather than perfecting one lets me communicate and understand more people."

### Topic 4: Ability & Disability

- **Personal Evidence:** Family photo and Eye Surgery (strabismus recovery at age 3-4, sitting upright wearing dark protective wraparound glasses).

- **Surface Perception / Interpretation:** Connecting the family photo with being temporarily blind after surgery, relying completely on caregivers.

- **Hidden Complication:** "Stress" and the "negatives of being blind and reliance on those around you." The fear of losing vision, being spoon-fed every meal, and being guided by the forearm everywhere. Realizing how exhausting it is for people with permanent disabilities in an ableist society.

- **Class Slide Connections:**

  * **Discussion about mental disability & Accommodation of disabilities:** How society treats accommodations as inconvenient burdens rather than basic rights, and the stigma around physical and mental disabilities.

  * `PARTICIPATION.pptx` (Slides 6 & 8): Positionality: Centering voices of disabled people vs. Consulting allies and planners (Transit accessibility scenario).

  * `ACCESS.pptx`: Physical and architectural barriers in public spaces, schools, and transit.

  * `Privilege and Oppression.pptx` (Slide 46): Stereotype #2: "People with disabilities are helpless or incapable", feeding the Cycle of Oppression and Internalization.

- **Personal Shift:** Moving from viewing disability as an isolated medical condition to understanding that ability and vulnerability exist on a continuous spectrum where everyone experiences reliance at some point.

- **Wondering Question (from draft sheet):** "At what point will society realize we're all on the spectrum?"

### Topic 5: Age

- **Personal Evidence:** Driver's licence (BC Class 7L), Student Gov (Grade 12 only -> proof of maturity), and young family photo vs. now showing growth throughout the years.

- **Surface Perception / Interpretation:** Turning 16, getting a driver's licence, and joining Grade 12 Student Gov are treated by institutions as proof of maturity and adult independence.

- **Hidden Complication:** "Parents withholding problem / info from me." When younger, parents hid financial and immigration stress to protect me. True maturity grew incrementally as they began sharing realities like being unable to buy a house even after eight years in Canada on a single income. The ageist double standard: "I am an adult if the situation needs me to be an adult, and a child otherwise."

- **Class Slide Connections:**

  * **Age dependence != Maturity discussion:** How legal and educational systems enforce dependency on youth based on age, confusing chronological age with actual maturity.

  * `Social Identity.pptx` (Slide 6): Age categories (Child, Teen, Young Adult, Adult, Senior) and how age is an assigned identity linked to power.

  * `Privilege and Oppression.pptx`: Ageism as an institutional power system where adults hold systemic authority. Slide 28: Privilege feels like ignorance.

  * `Why Injustice_.pptx` (Slides 10-11): Adolescent psychological needs for belonging, trust, and validation.

- **Personal Shift:** Rejecting bureaucratic milestones; realizing real maturity is about empathy, economic transparency, and supporting family through life's financial hurdles.

- **Wondering Question (from draft sheet):** "How does one determine maturity now that we know age has no relationship with it?"

### Topic 6: Gender

- **Personal Evidence:** Photos of me dressed up (black-and-cream patterned collared frock with pocket ribbons, age 2-3) and cousin dressed in the same clothes.

- **Surface Perception / Interpretation:** My cousin was born earlier than me, so my parents initially dressed me up with her clothes purely out of practical domestic frugality.

- **Hidden Complication:** "My cousin was born before me" and "Teased differently by relatives." At age two, it was seen as adorable, but as I grew older, relatives teased me differently, exposing how society conditions boys into rigid masculine boxes under threat of ridicule.

- **Class Slide Connections:**

  * **"The dress like the opposite sex" video (Gender vs. Sex experiment):** Class video where toddlers were dressed in clothes associated with the other gender to test unconscious bias. Nursery caregivers and adults instinctively handed dolls and tea sets to children dressed as girls, and construction trucks and blocks to children dressed as boys, doing so as second nature without realizing it. Demonstrates that while casual language says "opposite sex," this is fundamentally about **gender presentation and gender socialization**, as clothes and toys have no biological sex.

  * `Social Identity.pptx` (Slides 12 & 14): Biological Sex (male, female, intersex) vs. Socially Constructed Gender (cis, trans, non-binary, gender roles).

  * `Why Injustice_.pptx` (Slides 18-21): Nurture: Injustices and gender roles are learned behaviors taught from an early age; toddlers do not naturally police gender.

  * `Privilege and Oppression.pptx` (Slides 42-45): Cycle of Oppression and Internalization: Boys internalizing toxic masculinity out of a need for belonging.

- **Personal Shift:** Moving from childhood embarrassment about the photo to pride in seeing it as living proof that gender norms are socially constructed. Choosing casual hoodies over designer posturing reflects genuine self-worth grounded in character.

- **Wondering Question (from draft sheet):** "Does self-representation create the most memorable first impression? And what role does it play in interactions?"
